# Site 17 deterministic trajectory opportunity assessment

## Executive decision

No neural network was trained and no existing artifact was modified. The three saved `MEAN_ONLY / TRAJECTORY_ONLY` checkpoints were used only for deterministic inference on the already-defined Train and Validation windows; the saved 2022 Test arrays were recomputed only as an explicitly non-independent, previously inspected exploratory check.

The largest reproducible failure is **trajectory change-amplitude collapse under high-variation daylight conditions**. It is not primarily a global amplitude bias and is not consistently a simple time shift. Validation predictions retain nearly the correct marginal level variance (prediction/truth standard-deviation ratio about 0.970 at H12), yet their step-change amplitude is only 0.377 of truth and their aggregate total variation is 0.437 of truth. High-change windows account for 97.6% of Validation squared error and midday windows for 63.9%.

Exactly one next-stage candidate is selected: **Anchored Level–Increment Consistency Decoder (ALICD)**. It keeps the clean protocol and ModernTCN backbone, replaces only the direct `Linear(4608,12)` head, predicts sparse level anchors and all twelve increments, and uses a fixed differentiable least-squares consistency projection so cumulative increments agree with anchors. This is a narrow test of whether the existing representation contains trajectory-shape information that the unconstrained direct head smooths away. It is not another attention, gate, ODE, event model, or encoder stack.

ALICD has only *potential* ASOC novelty. Anchor prediction, derivative losses, multi-scale forecasting and shape losses all have strong precedents. Any eventual novelty would have to reside in the explicit anchor–increment reconciliation operator, its causal PV formulation, and robust experimental evidence—not in its component names. One three-seed candidate screen (3 new trainings, baseline reused) is justified; if it fails, stop without v2/v3.

## Failure-type decomposition

### Magnitude and energy

Validation mean trajectory bias is small and seed-dependent (−0.026 to +0.017 kW), whereas peak power is consistently underestimated by 0.115–0.159 kW. H12 energy-sum error changes sign across seeds. A scalar residual/bias correction therefore does not target the stable failure.

### Timing

Non-circular lags −3…+3 were evaluated using only overlapping points. The dominant best lag changes between −3 and +3 across seeds, mean best lag ranges from −0.17 to +1.02 steps, and alignment reduces mean per-window RMSE by only 0.046–0.051 kW. Timing error exists locally, but there is no stable global advance/lag that supports an interpolation or temporal-shift correction.

### Over-smoothing

Validation predicted/true aggregate total-variation ratios are 0.429–0.447; peak-rate ratios are 0.409–0.468. This is the strongest seed-stable structural defect. Marginal output standard deviation near 0.97 shows that the model can reproduce the power-level distribution while failing to reproduce within-trajectory dynamics.

### Direction

Direction tolerance is fixed from the Train distribution at 0.03640 kW (25th percentile of nonzero Train step changes). Full per-seed counts for rise→fall, fall→rise, change→flat and flat→false-change are in the CSV. Direction agreement is only about 0.36 beyond H1, consistent with smoothed and weakly oriented steps.

## Can past information identify large errors?

Yes, at a regime level. A fixed Train-only histogram gradient-boosting explainer using forecast-origin-or-earlier variables explains Validation window-RMSE variation with R² 0.692–0.709 and reduces absolute error-of-error from roughly 0.200–0.202 kW to 0.102–0.106 kW. It is an explanatory diagnostic, not a forecasting candidate.

The strongest mean Validation Spearman correlations with window RMSE are:

- past irradiance standard deviation: 0.815;
- recent six-step power range: 0.793;
- time-of-day cosine: −0.791;
- MB0/MB1 five-minute irradiance means: 0.727/0.724;
- current power: 0.706.

Recent missingness is only 0.028 and minimum valid fraction −0.011. Temperature and humidity are **not present in the actual `MEAN_ONLY` prepared tensor**, so no fabricated association is reported. They exist in the broader raw PV CSV but would require a new clean-protocol feature decision in a future experiment.

The prior high-frequency feature experiment failed not because volatility is unrelated to error: volatility clearly marks difficult daylight regimes. It failed because correlation with forecast difficulty is not incremental causal information about the sign and magnitude of future cloud evolution. The failed `HF_DYNAMICS` point model added past std/range/slope but worsened H12 in all seeds. Past volatility says “this origin is risky”; it does not reveal which unobserved cloud transition will occur after the origin. The remaining error is therefore a mixture: a strongly predictable **structural smoothing bias** plus irreducible/future-weather uncertainty.

## Literature and novelty implications

The closest primary sources are listed in `LITERATURE_SOURCES.md`. The decisive findings are:

- TimeMixer and PV-specific ADAMS/decomposition papers already occupy generic multiscale/coarse-to-fine territory.
- DILATE, TILDE-Q and SATL occupy generic shape, temporal-distortion, derivative and frequency-loss claims.
- PLSTNet and a 2026 dual-branch PV framework occupy trend/fluctuation or baseline/ramp parallel branches.
- Clear-sky-index and multi-fidelity PV frameworks already exist and require inputs/physics not currently verified here.
- ModernTCN is an ICLR 2024 baseline and cannot itself be claimed as project innovation.

Accordingly, “add attention”, “add a gate”, “add derivative loss”, “add FFT loss”, “add a ramp branch”, and “add multiple resolutions” are rejected as stand-alone candidate claims.

## Unique next-stage candidate: ALICD

**Name:** Anchored Level–Increment Consistency Decoder.

**Scientific question:** Is the measured H2–H12 trajectory smoothing caused partly by the unconstrained direct linear decoder, such that explicitly reconciling level anchors and increments restores variation without degrading level RMSE?

**Input:** Exactly the existing 72×14 `MEAN_ONLY` causal tensor; no future irradiance, new weather, event label or Test-derived feature.

**Structure:** Keep the 64-channel, four-block ModernTCN backbone. Replace only `Linear(4608,12)` with (i) a 12-increment linear head and (ii) a sparse level-anchor head at H3/H6/H12. Convert increments to a trajectory anchored at the last observed power, then apply a fixed differentiable minimum-norm correction that makes the cumulative trajectory agree with the predicted anchors. No new encoder, attention, gate or recurrence.

**Loss:** standardized H12 level MSE plus a fixed, predeclared coefficient on first-difference MSE. Anchor supervision is level MSE at H3/H6/H12. The coefficient must be written once before training and not searched; the direct baseline retains its original loss. Evaluation, not selection, includes total variation and direction metrics.

**Only structural difference from baseline:** the H12 linear output head is replaced by the anchor/increment heads and deterministic reconciliation operator. Backbone, data, optimizer, seeds and training budget remain unchanged.

**Expected target:** Validation high-change and midday RMSE, H3/H6/H12 RMSE, difference MAE, aggregate TV ratio, peak-rate ratio and direction accuracy. Success would require a material TV/difference recovery without a compensating full/daylight RMSE degradation, assessed consistently across seeds—not a new automatic gate.

**Why it may work:** the backbone output evidently retains power-level information, while the current head produces trajectories with only 37.7% of true H12 step amplitude. Direct increment supervision provides a local shape signal, and anchors prevent cumulative drift.

**Why it may fail:** future cloud motion is unobserved; forcing variation can manufacture oscillations. Prior event and high-frequency feature experiments show that adding auxiliary objectives can degrade power RMSE. The reconciliation may merely redistribute error.

**Minimum execution:** 3 ALICD seeds (42/43/44); reuse the existing three baseline artifacts. This is below the six-run ceiling. No hyperparameter search and no second candidate.

**Implementation blueprint:**

1. New isolated screen directory; import clean preparation and ModernTCN backbone.
2. Freeze the protocol/config before Test evaluation; derive no model choice from Test.
3. Implement `AnchoredIncrementDecoder` and a closed-form reconciliation unit test.
4. Tests: output shape, exact causal origins, Train-only preprocessing, anchor consistency, gradient flow, no Test loader in training, identical labels/timestamps, and metric recomputation.
5. Train three seeds with the existing optimizer/epochs/patience and Validation-only checkpointing.
6. Compare against reused baseline on Validation first; report Test only as exploratory because 2022 Test is already exhausted.
7. If shape metrics improve but RMSE worsens, or gains are seed-specific, terminate without v2/v3.

**Potential ASOC contribution:** conditional and not yet established. ALICD is more defensible than NODE, Ramp events or module stacking because it is derived from a measured decoder-level structural defect and makes a falsifiable level–shape consistency claim. It is still only a candidate until it beats the direct head and subsequently survives a genuinely untouched later-period confirmation set.

## Final answers

1. Largest failure: high-change daylight trajectory smoothing.
2. Primary type: over-smoothing/change-amplitude collapse; timing and scalar bias are secondary.
3. Dominant contributors: H9–H12, high-change, midday and high-power windows.
4. Past information predicts error *risk* well, but not necessarily future change direction.
5. HF statistics mark volatile regimes but do not supply future cloud evolution; the prior encoding added no stable point-forecast gain.
6. Reuse clean preparation, windowing, ModernTCN backbone, Validation checkpointing and metric utilities only.
7. Closest papers: ModernTCN, TimeMixer, ADAMS, PLSTNet, SATL/DILATE/TILDE-Q and recent multi-scale/ramp PV frameworks.
8. Already occupied: generic multi-scale, decomposition, derivative/FFT/shape loss, clear-sky residual, ramp branch and forecast blending.
9. Unique candidate: ALICD.
10. It targets a measured direct-decoder defect rather than adding generic complexity.
11. Yes: one three-seed screen is warranted, maximum three new trainings.
12. This is not a declaration of a new ASOC method; failure should yield NO-CANDIDATE and terminate iteration.
